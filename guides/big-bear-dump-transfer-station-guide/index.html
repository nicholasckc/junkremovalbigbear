Big Bear Transfer Station location, hours, accepted and banned items and county fees, plus bulky pickup, Clean Bear, HHW and e-waste options by area.

Where is the dump in Big Bear?

The Big Bear Transfer Station is at 38550 Holcomb Valley Road, Big Bear City, CA 92314, open Monday–Saturday 8 AM–4:30 PM (closed Sunday). San Bernardino County runs it. County fees are weight-based, at $13.71 for a residential load up to 300 lb and $61.49 per ton prorated above that (pricing effective July 1, 2023). Uncovered loads pay double. It doesn't take paint, hazardous waste, liquids, items over 4 feet long or treated wood. For bulky items, check your area's curbside program first, or have it hauled.

The answer depends on what it is and where the property is, because the City of Big Bear Lake and the unincorporated areas (Big Bear City, Sugarloaf, Erwin Lake, Baldwin Lake, Fawnskin) use different providers. The Big Bear Transfer Station

The valley's public drop-off is the Big Bear Transfer Station, 38550 Holcomb Valley Road, Big Bear City, CA 92314, north of Baldwin Lake off Highway 18. It's open Monday–Saturday, 8 AM to 4:30 PM and closed Sunday. San Bernardino County Public Works runs it, and county-wide rules apply.

Accepted, with conditions

  • Household junk, furniture and construction debris (cut to under 4 feet)
  • Green waste. Big Bear City CSD customers can self-haul it with their Disposal Use Permit at no extra cost
  • Tires: up to 9, off the rim, for a per-tire fee
  • Refrigerators and freezers: only once the Freon has been removed

Not accepted

  • Paint, hazardous waste, liquids and sealed containers
  • Hot ashes, mercury- and lead-containing items, medical waste and sharps
  • Items over 4 feet long (county landfills take up to 10 feet)
  • Treated wood (only the county's Mid-Valley and Victorville sites take it)
  • CRT TVs and monitors at sites without a scale house; use the county's free TV and monitor drop-off instead
  • Waste from outside San Bernardino County
  • Asbestos: demolition waste from structures built before 1984 needs certification, and friable asbestos goes only to specific out-of-county landfills

Add your own costs: a truck or trailer, a tarp and straps, fuel for the round trip, and your time. For one load of loose bags, the transfer station is hard to beat. For a sofa, a fridge and half a garage, compare a haul-away quote first.

Step-by-step: doing a dump run

  1. Check whether a free or curbside option fits first

    City of Big Bear Lake addresses get one free curbside bulky pickup a year from Big Bear Disposal. Big Bear City CSD areas get curbside bulky pickup for a per-item fee. If your load is a couch and a mattress, that may be all you need.

  2. Pull out everything the station won't take

    Paint, chemicals, fuels, propane cylinders, batteries, fluorescent tubes, liquids, sealed containers, hot ashes and medical waste go elsewhere (mostly HHW). So do treated wood (Mid-Valley and Victorville only) and electronics. Fridges and freezers must have the Freon removed first.

  3. Cut long items down to under 4 feet

    County transfer stations don't accept items over 4 feet long. Cut lumber, fencing, carpet rolls and pipe to length at home, where you have room and tools.

  4. Load heavy items low and forward, then tarp and strap

    Put appliances and dense loads against the cab, lighter bulky items on top, and cover the whole load with a tarp tied at every corner. The county charges double for uncovered or unsecured loads, and loose debris on Highway 18 or Big Bear Blvd is dangerous.

  5. Go early in the week, not at closing

    The station is open Monday–Saturday, 8 AM–4:30 PM. Arrive with time to unload before closing, and check the county page for holiday closures and weather closures before you drive.

  6. Weigh in, unload where directed, keep the receipt

    Follow the attendant's directions for where each material goes. Fees are based on the load. Keep your receipt if you're clearing a rental, an estate or a property you manage.

Curbside and bulky pickup, by area

  • City of Big Bear Lake (92315): Big Bear Disposal gives each residence one free curbside bulky-item pickup per year (up to 3 cubic yards or 4 large items); extra pickups cost a fee. They also offer a free 96-gallon green cart and a 4-yard Rent-a-Bin. See Big Bear Disposal residential services.
  • Big Bear City, Sugarloaf, Erwin Lake, Baldwin Lake: the Big Bear City CSD collects household bulky items curbside for a per-item fee. Construction debris, tires, electronics, carpet and metal aren't accepted curbside. Carts can go out no more than 12 hours before collection and must be back within 24 hours after. See the CSD residential collection page.
  • Community clean-up days (CSD): the 2026 clean-up days (April 25, August 1 and October 10, at the Paradise Maintenance Yard, 417 Grenfall Ln) have passed. The CSD lists a green-waste-only drop-off day on Saturday, November 14, 2026, 9 AM–2 PM.
  • Fawnskin and other unincorporated areas: residential service in Fawnskin is optional through Big Bear Disposal; check with your provider.

The Clean Bear Site (City of Big Bear Lake)

The City and Big Bear Disposal run one drop-off: Clean Bear Site #1, 41970 Garstin Drive, open 7 AM–8 PM daily. It's for City of Big Bear Lake residents who can't use weekly curbside service, and for visitors staying at a city address. Bring proof of eligibility: photo ID, a property document, a utility bill, car registration, or (for visitors) a rental contract for a city address. It has bins for household trash, recycling, green waste, e-waste, universal waste and ashes (soaked for at least 48 hours). Clean Bear Site #2 on Big Bear Blvd closed on August 2, 2026. There's no bulky-item bin, and contractor green waste isn't allowed.

Hazardous waste, e-waste and the "can't go anywhere" list

California bans these from the trash: electronics (TVs, monitors, laptops, microwaves, phones), batteries, fluorescent tubes, paint, propane cylinders, treated wood and asbestos. San Bernardino County Fire runs the household hazardous waste (HHW) program. Its Big Bear collection site is at the City of Big Bear Lake Public Works Yard, 42040 Garstin Dr, normally Saturdays 9 AM–2 PM. Check the HHW page for closures. Our trucks don't take hazardous waste either.

Bear country and cart rules

  • Keep trash inside or in a bear-resistant container until collection day. Big Bear Disposal offers bear-resistant carts for a one-time fee.
  • Follow cart timing: the CSD allows carts out no more than 12 hours before collection and back in within 24 hours after, and the City prohibits leaving cans at the curb for more than 24 hours.
  • Vacation rentals: guests overflow bins fast on holiday weekends. Read the Big Bear turnover checklist.

When a haul-away beats a dump run

If the job includes heavy furniture, appliances, stairs, a whole room or more, or items the station won't take as-is (long lumber, a fridge with Freon), the real cost of doing it yourself goes up fast. You're paying for a truck, fees and a lost day. We load from wherever it sits and deliver to a facility that accepts it, with the price confirmed before we start. Frequently asked questions

Is the Big Bear dump open on Sunday?

No. The Big Bear Transfer Station, 38550 Holcomb Valley Rd, is open Monday–Saturday 8 AM–4:30 PM and closed Sunday. Check San Bernardino County's disposal sites page for holiday or weather closures.

How much does the Big Bear dump cost?

San Bernardino County lists $13.71 for a residential load up to 300 lb and $61.49 per ton, prorated, for 301 lb or more, plus $5.31 per tire (up to 9). Uncovered or unsecured loads pay double. This is pricing effective July 1, 2023; check the county's current fee schedule before you go.

Does Big Bear have free bulky item pickup?

In the City of Big Bear Lake, Big Bear Disposal gives each residence one free curbside bulky-item pickup per year (up to 3 cubic yards or 4 large items). In Big Bear City, Sugarloaf and Erwin Lake, the Big Bear City CSD picks up bulky items for a per-item fee.

Can I take a fridge to the Big Bear Transfer Station?

Only once the Freon has been removed. San Bernardino County sites don't accept refrigerators that still contain refrigerant.

Can I take pressure-treated wood to the Big Bear dump?

No. San Bernardino County accepts treated wood only at its Mid-Valley and Victorville sites.

Where do I take paint and propane tanks in Big Bear?

To the San Bernardino County Fire household hazardous waste program. Its Big Bear collection site is at the City of Big Bear Lake Public Works Yard, 42040 Garstin Dr, normally Saturdays 9 AM–2 PM. Check the HHW page for current hours.

Should I do a dump run or hire junk removal?

For a single small load you can lift, the transfer station is cheapest. For heavy, bulky or multi-item loads, stairs, or items with special rules, a haul-away is often comparable once fees, a truck and your time are counted. A free quote makes it easy to compare.
