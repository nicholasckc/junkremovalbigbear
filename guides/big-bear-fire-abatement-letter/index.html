Got a Big Bear Fire Abatement Letter? Here's Exactly What to Do

Got a weed abatement notice from Big Bear Fire? What the letter means, the clearance rules, deadlines, and how to pass inspection the first time.

What should I do if I receive a weed abatement notice in Big Bear?

Treat the letter as your official warning — Big Bear Fire Department is not required to send a separate violation notice before issuing a citation. Clear hazardous vegetation to the Priority Defensible Space standards (or hire help), keep photo documentation, and verify requirements on Big Bear Fire's website. Why you got the letter

Each year, Big Bear Fire Department mails abatement notices to property owners across the Valley — occupied homes and vacant lots alike. It isn't personal, and it doesn't necessarily mean your property was flagged as a problem: the notice states the defensible space standards every parcel must meet before fire season peaks. What makes it serious is the fine print: the notification letter is your warning. The department is not obligated to issue a separate notice of violation before citing you.

What the Fire Department expects, in plain English

  • 0–5 ft from structures: no pine needles, forest litter, dead vegetation, or combustible mulch — bare, ember-resistant ground
  • Within 15 ft: dry brush and high-energy-release vegetation removed near structures, propane tanks, hydrants and wood fencing
  • Whole property: forest litter thinned to about 2 inches depth (a thin layer stays for erosion control)
  • Roofs, gutters & decks: pine needles cleared from roofing, gutters, under and on decking, stairs and parking pads
  • Trees under 45 ft: lower limbs pruned up to 6 ft (max 25% of tree height)
  • Trees over 45 ft: limbed 12–15 ft up, and nothing within 10 ft of chimney openings

The full standards are on bigbearfire.org, and the department takes requests for defensible-space (AB 38) inspections — check its site to confirm current requirements for your parcel. Fawnskin (92333) is different: weed abatement and AB 38 inspections there are handled by San Bernardino County Fire, not Big Bear Fire. If cost is the obstacle, Big Bear Fire points owners to the Mountain Rim Fire Safe Council (firesafenow.org), which has had grant funding to help some owners with abatement notices — an application does not guarantee approval.

Your three options

Do it yourself. Doable for a small, flat lot — but budget full weekends, a truck for hauling, and transfer-station trips. The most common DIY failure isn't the clearing, it's the debris: piles left on the property don't count as abatement.

Hire a mow-and-blow crew. Cheaper per hour, but most leave the cut brush at your curb. You still own the hauling problem — and the deadline.

Hire a clearing + hauling service. One visit: cut, rake, prune, load, gone. This is what we do — and because we're a hauling company first, the debris leaves on our truck the same day.

If you're not on the mountain

A huge share of abatement notices go to second-home owners down the hill. You don't need to drive up: forward us the letter, send a few property photos on WhatsApp, approve the quote remotely, and you'll get before-and-after photos when the lot passes muster. Keep those photos — they're your proof of compliance if questions ever come up.

Don't wait for the inspection window

Abatement season compresses fast once letters land: crews book out, and the closer to inspection you start, the fewer options you have. The cheapest and calmest time to handle an abatement letter is the week it arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Is the abatement letter a citation?

No — it's the official warning that precedes one. Big Bear Fire is not required to send a separate notice of violation before citing a non-compliant property, so the letter itself is your deadline.

Do vacant lots have to be cleared too?

Yes. Priority defensible space requirements apply to vacant and unbuilt parcels as well as occupied properties, and vacant-lot owners receive the same notices.
