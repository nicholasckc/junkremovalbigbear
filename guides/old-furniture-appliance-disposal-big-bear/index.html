What to Do With Old Furniture and Appliances in Big Bear

Where old couches, mattresses, fridges, washers and TVs can go in Big Bear and the San Bernardino Mountains: pickup programs, county rules, Freon and e-waste.

How do I get rid of an old couch or fridge in Big Bear?

Start with the free and low-cost programs. In the City of Big Bear Lake, Big Bear Disposal gives each residence one free curbside bulky pickup a year (up to 3 cubic yards or 4 large items). In Big Bear City, Sugarloaf and Erwin Lake, the Big Bear City CSD picks up bulky items for a per-item fee. Refrigerators and freezers are only accepted at county sites once the Freon is out. TVs, monitors and microwaves are e-waste and can't go in the trash. For heavy items, stairs or several pieces at once, a haul-away crew is usually simpler.

Big Bear has more heavy furniture per square foot than most places. There are log-frame bunk beds, sleeper sofas hauled up decades ago, and fridges in the garage that have outlived three owners. Getting rid of them is mostly about knowing which program fits your address and which rules apply to each item type. This guide covers the whole Big Bear Valley and the nearby San Bernardino Mountains communities that use the Big Bear Transfer Station.

Step-by-step: getting rid of furniture and appliances

  1. List every item and what it's made of

    Write down each piece: sofa, sleeper sofa (they contain a heavy steel frame), mattress and box spring, dresser, fridge, washer, dryer, range, microwave, TV. The item type decides where it can legally go, so sort by type before you think about logistics.

  2. Try sell, donate or give away first

    Solid wood furniture, working appliances and clean mattresses still have value. Post them locally with clear photos and a pickup-only note. Most charities won't take stained upholstery, mattresses or appliances that don't work, so call before you load up.

  3. Ask about retailer take-back

    Buying a replacement? California requires retailers that deliver a new mattress to offer to take back the old one (see Bye Bye Mattress). Many appliance stores offer haul-away at delivery too. Ask when you order, because adding it later is harder.

  4. Book your area's bulky pickup

    City of Big Bear Lake (92315): schedule the free annual bulky pickup with Big Bear Disposal; extra pickups cost a fee. Big Bear City, Sugarloaf, Erwin Lake and Baldwin Lake: book Big Bear City CSD curbside bulky pickup (per-item fee). Read the list of what each program won't take before you drag anything to the curb.

  5. Prepare appliances properly

    Unplug and empty fridges and freezers; defrost and dry them, and tape the doors shut for transport. A fridge or freezer can only go to a county site once the refrigerant (Freon) has been removed. Disconnect washers and dryers at the valves, drain the hoses, and leave gas appliances to a qualified technician to disconnect and cap.

  6. Separate the e-waste

    TVs, monitors, computers, microwaves and anything with a battery or circuit board are e-waste or universal waste in California and can't go in the trash. Old CRT tube TVs are heavy and aren't accepted at county sites without a scale house. Use the county HHW program's free TV and monitor drop-off, or the Clean Bear Site's e-waste bin if you're an eligible City of Big Bear Lake resident.

  7. Self-haul to the transfer station, or have it hauled

    The Big Bear Transfer Station is at 38550 Holcomb Valley Rd, Big Bear City, open Monday–Saturday 8 AM–4:30 PM. Tarp and tie down the load (uncovered loads pay double), and expect weight-based fees. If the pieces are upstairs, oversized or more than one trip, a haul-away service saves the lifting and the round trips.

Item by item: what's allowed where

Sofas, recliners, sleeper sofas and sectionals

These are all bulky items, eligible for the curbside programs above. Sleeper sofas and power recliners are far heavier than they look because of the steel mechanism. If you're taking one apart to fit it in a truck, remember that county transfer stations don't accept items over 4 feet long. Strap everything down: loose cushions blowing off a truck on Highway 18 are a hazard, and county sites charge double for uncovered or unsecured loads.

Mattresses and box springs

California runs a statewide mattress recycling program (Bye Bye Mattress). Retailers delivering a new mattress have to offer to take back the old one, so ask at purchase. Otherwise, mattresses count as bulky items for curbside pickup. During a cabin turnover, wrap mattresses in plastic before carrying them through the cabin, especially if bedbugs are a concern.

Dressers, tables, bunk beds and bed frames

Solid wood sells or donates well. Take out drawers and unbolt bunk beds before you move them, because a full dresser is a two-person carry on stairs. Particleboard pieces that are already broken are trash or bulky waste; they rarely survive a second move.

Refrigerators, freezers, wine fridges and AC units

These hold refrigerant. San Bernardino County sites accept refrigerators only once the Freon has been removed. California also treats appliances as containing "materials that require special handling", which have to be removed before the appliance is crushed or recycled. Don't cut refrigerant lines yourself. A bulky pickup program, an appliance retailer's haul-away or a hauler that delivers to a facility that processes appliances is the practical route.

Washers, dryers, ranges and dishwashers

These are metal appliances, accepted as bulky items. Disconnect water at the valves and drain the hoses into a bucket first. A gas dryer or range must be disconnected and capped by someone qualified. Never leave an open gas line.

TVs, monitors, microwaves and electronics

California bans electronics from the trash, including TVs, monitors, laptops, microwaves and phones. County sites without a scale house don't accept old CRT tube TVs or monitors. The county's HHW program offers free TV and monitor drop-off. Eligible City of Big Bear Lake residents can use the e-waste bin at the Clean Bear Site.

Water heaters, propane grills and anything with fuel

Drain water heaters fully. For grills, disconnect and remove the propane cylinder; cylinders are hazardous waste and go to HHW. We don't haul propane tanks, fuel or chemicals.

Bear country and snow: local logistics

  • Don't leave a fridge at the curb with food smells. Clean it out and air it inside. A curbside fridge with residue is an invitation to bears.
  • Snow and ice. From late fall to spring, carry routes over decks and outdoor stairs can be icy. Clear and salt the path before moving day, and don't plan a two-person stair carry on a storm day.
  • Narrow streets. Many Big Bear streets have no shoulder. Leave room for plows and emergency vehicles when you stage items for pickup, and follow your provider's timing rules for when carts and items can go out.

Frequently asked questions

Does Big Bear have free furniture pickup?

Yes, in the City of Big Bear Lake. Big Bear Disposal gives each residence one free curbside bulky-item pickup per year, up to 3 cubic yards or 4 large items; extra pickups cost a fee. In Big Bear City, Sugarloaf and Erwin Lake, the Big Bear City CSD picks up bulky items for a per-item fee.

Can I take a refrigerator to the Big Bear dump?

San Bernardino County sites accept refrigerators only once the Freon has been removed. Have the refrigerant removed by a qualified service first, or use a bulky pickup program, retailer haul-away or a hauler that delivers to an appliance processor.

Where can I get rid of an old TV in Big Bear?

Not in the trash: California bans electronics from household trash. The San Bernardino County Fire HHW program offers free TV and monitor drop-off. Eligible City of Big Bear Lake residents can use the e-waste bin at the Clean Bear Site, 41970 Garstin Dr.

How do I get rid of a mattress in Big Bear?

If you're buying a new one, ask the retailer to take the old one back; California's mattress program requires delivering retailers to offer this. Otherwise, use your bulky pickup program, take it to the Big Bear Transfer Station, or have it hauled.

Can I leave furniture at the Clean Bear Site?

No. The Clean Bear Site has bins for household trash, recycling, green waste, e-waste, universal waste and soaked ashes only. Furniture and appliances need a bulky pickup, a transfer-station trip or a haul-away service.

Will you carry furniture down from a loft or upstairs bedroom?

Yes. Carrying items out from wherever they sit (lofts, upstairs bedrooms, decks, garages) is the normal job for us in Big Bear. Text a photo of the item and the stairs to (425) 233-2945 for a firm price.
