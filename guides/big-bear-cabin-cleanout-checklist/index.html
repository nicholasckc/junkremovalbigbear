Big Bear Cabin Cleanout Checklist: A Room-by-Room Plan

A room-by-room Big Bear cabin cleanout checklist: sorting, bear-safe staging, hazardous items, where each pile goes under local rules, and cost factors.

How do you clean out a Big Bear cabin?

Pick a deadline and check winter access first, then work one room at a time into six piles: keep, sell, donate, recycle, hazardous and haul. Hazardous items go to the county HHW site, not the trash. Everything else goes through your area's curbside program (Big Bear Disposal in the City of Big Bear Lake, the Big Bear City CSD elsewhere), the Big Bear Transfer Station, or a haul-away crew. Stage bags inside, not outside, because of bears.

Big Bear cabins fill up in a particular way. There are decades of board games and bunk-bed mattresses, a garage of skis and propane bottles, and a deck stacked with snow-worn furniture. On top of that, the property may have changed hands three times, with each owner adding a layer. This checklist is the order we'd work in. It's written so you can do it yourself, call us in for just the heavy part, or hand the whole job over.

Step-by-step: how to clean out a cabin

  1. Set the scope, the deadline and who decides

    Write down what "done" means: empty for a sale, ready for renters, or just the garage and loft. Agree on who has the final say on keepsakes before anyone starts sorting. Sorting stalls far more often over decisions than over effort.

  2. Check access before you book anything

    Mountain access decides how long everything takes. Note the driveway grade, the number of steps to the door, where a truck can park on narrow streets, and whether snow or ice will be on the path. From November to March, plan around storms and plowing, and keep a shovel and ice melt by the door.

  3. Set up six sorting zones

    Use tape or signs to set up Keep, Sell, Donate, Recycle, Hazardous and Haul. Put the haul zone nearest the exit. Put hazardous items on a tarp in a garage or shed, away from kids and pets, and never next to the woodstove.

  4. Work room by room, top to bottom

    Start with the room that frees up the most space (usually the garage or a spare bedroom). Clear high shelves and lofts first so debris falls onto areas you haven't cleaned yet. Use the room checklist below so nothing gets missed.

  5. Pull hazardous items out early

    Paint, propane cylinders, fuel, pesticides, pool and spa chemicals, batteries and fluorescent tubes can't go in a trash cart or a regular transfer-station load. Box them separately for the county HHW program (details below).

  6. Keep food waste and trash bear-safe

    Big Bear is bear country. Empty the fridge and pantry last, the day before a trash collection, and keep bagged trash inside or in a bear-resistant container until the truck comes. Never leave open bags on a deck or at the curb overnight.

  7. Route each pile to the right place

    Book your free or low-cost options first: the annual bulky pickup in the City of Big Bear Lake, per-item bulky pickup from the Big Bear City CSD, or a run to the Big Bear Transfer Station. Use a haul-away service for what's left, especially heavy furniture, appliances and anything stuck upstairs.

  8. Do the final sweep and take photos

    Check under beds, behind the water heater, in the crawlspace and the attic hatch. Take dated photos of every room for the family, the agent or the property manager. If you're selling, now is a good time to clear the lot for defensible space too.

Room-by-room checklist

Print this or keep it open on your phone. Tick each line off as the room clears.

Kitchen and pantry

  • Food: donate sealed, in-date items; bag the rest and keep it indoors until collection day (bears)
  • Fridge and freezer: empty, wipe and leave the doors propped open to air out. Moving or disposing of the appliance has rules; see furniture and appliance disposal
  • Small appliances: microwaves, toasters and coffee makers. Microwaves count as electronic waste in California, so they go to e-waste, not the trash
  • Duplicate cookware, mismatched mugs, old cleaning products (any that are hazardous go in the HHW box)

Living room and bedrooms

  • Sofas, recliners, bunk beds and mattresses: decide early, because these drive the size of the haul
  • Bedding and linens: donate clean sets; worn ones are trash
  • TVs, DVD players, old stereos and cables: e-waste
  • Papers: shred anything with account numbers, tax info or signatures; keep deeds, titles and warranties in one box

Bathrooms

  • Expired medicines and sharps: never in the trash. Ask the county HHW program or a pharmacy take-back
  • Half-used aerosols and cleaners: HHW box
  • Towels, bath mats and shower curtains: trash or textile donation

Garage, shed and under-cabin storage

  • Propane cylinders, gas cans, motor oil, paint, stains and pesticides: HHW, not trash and not a haul-away truck
  • Old skis, sleds, bikes and fishing gear: sell or donate if usable
  • Scrap lumber and broken furniture: pieces over 4 feet long aren't accepted at county transfer stations, so cut them down or have them hauled
  • Tires: county sites take up to 9 per trip, off the rim, for a per-tire fee
  • The shed itself? See shed removal in Big Bear

Deck, yard and lot

  • Snow-cracked patio furniture, rusted grills (empty and disconnect the propane first), dead planters
  • A hot tub that no longer works: see how to get rid of an old hot tub
  • Pine needles, branches and firewood stacked against the cabin: a fire risk, and Big Bear Fire inspects for it. See weed abatement and defensible space

Before and after: what "clean" looks like

For a sale or a new listing, aim for clear floors and surfaces, one piece of furniture per purpose, and empty closets that show their size. For a family cabin you're keeping, the goal is simpler: nothing broken, nothing expired, and nothing stored against the walls where it can hide mice or hold moisture. If the cabin is going on the market, read how to declutter a cabin before selling or renting.

What a cabin cleanout costs: the real factors

We don't publish flat prices, because two cabins with the same square footage can be completely different jobs. These are the things that move the cost up or down, whether you do it yourself or hire it out:

  • Volume and weight. County transfer stations charge by weight: a flat fee up to 300 lb, then a per-ton rate (see the table in our dump guide). Appliances, tile, wet carpet and books weigh more than they look.
  • Item types. Tires, refrigerators, e-waste and hazardous items each have their own rules and fees, and some have to go to a different place.
  • Access. Stairs, a steep or icy driveway, a long carry from the street, or a loft ladder all add labor time.
  • Free programs you can still use. Use the annual free bulky pickup (City of Big Bear Lake) if you haven't already this year. Check the Big Bear City CSD community clean-up days too. The 2026 general clean-up days have passed, but the CSD lists a green-waste-only drop-off day on November 14, 2026.
  • Your time and vehicle. A pickup, tie-downs and a tarp (uncovered loads pay double at county sites), plus round trips to Holcomb Valley Road.

With us, the price is confirmed before any work starts, after a free on-site look or from photos you text us.

Owners who live off the mountain

Many Big Bear cabins are second homes. If you're driving up from Orange County or Los Angeles for a weekend, do the deciding remotely. Frequently asked questions

How long does a cabin cleanout take?

It depends on how full the cabin is and how quickly decisions get made. Sorting usually takes longer than hauling. A single garage can take an afternoon; a fully furnished multi-level cabin can take a few days of sorting before the haul. Making keep-or-go decisions in advance (photos, colored stickers) is the biggest time saver.

Can I put furniture at the Clean Bear Site?

No. The City of Big Bear Lake Clean Bear Site (41970 Garstin Dr) has bins for household trash, recycling, green waste, e-waste, universal waste and soaked ashes. There is no bulky-item bin. Use the annual Big Bear Disposal bulky pickup, the Big Bear Transfer Station, or a haul-away service for furniture.

What can't go in the trash during a cleanout?

In California, electronics (TVs, monitors, computers, microwaves, phones), batteries, fluorescent tubes, paint, propane cylinders, treated wood and asbestos can't go in the trash. Take them to the San Bernardino County Fire HHW program or the right e-waste option.

Do you take hazardous items like paint and propane?

No. We don't haul paint, chemicals, motor oil, fuels, asbestos or propane tanks. Box them separately and take them to the county HHW collection site. We take almost everything else, from furniture and appliances to garage contents and deck debris.

Do I need to be there for the cleanout?

No. Many of our customers own Big Bear cabins but live off the mountain. We can work from a lockbox or gate code, follow a list or colored stickers, and send before-and-after photos when we finish.
