How to Remove an Old Shed in Big Bear (and What the Rules Say)

How to remove an old shed or small deck section in Big Bear: permit checks, asbestos and treated-wood rules, safe tear-down steps, disposal and cost factors.

Do I need a permit to tear down a shed in Big Bear?

It depends on the shed and where it is. In unincorporated San Bernardino County (Big Bear City, Sugarloaf, Fawnskin, Erwin Lake), a one-story shed up to 120 sq ft with no utilities can be built without a building permit. Demolishing a building that was permitted generally needs a demolition permit, and county disposal sites require asbestos certification for demolition waste from structures built before 1984. Inside the City of Big Bear Lake, check with the City. Always empty the shed of hazardous items first, cut debris to under 4 feet for the transfer station, and remember that treated wood can't go to the Big Bear Transfer Station.

Big Bear is hard on sheds. Snow load sags roofs, freeze-thaw cycles rot the floor framing, and pine needles pile up on the roof and against the walls. A lot of the sheds we see were built decades ago and have changed hands with the cabin. Before you pull one down, sort out three things: who issues permits for your address, what the shed is made of, and where each kind of debris is allowed to go. The same rules cover small deck sections and fencing.

What we do and don't do. Junk Removal Big Bear is not a licensed contractor. Painting, light demolition and similar work is limited to minor jobs under $1,000 total (labor and materials) that don't need a building permit, as California law allows. We take down and haul small, non-structural sheds, playsets, deck boards and short fence runs that qualify as minor work. Anything bigger, structural or permitted needs a licensed demolition contractor and, often, a permit.

Step-by-step: removing a shed safely

  1. Find out what you have

    Measure the footprint and note the construction: wood frame, metal kit or resin. Check whether it has power, water or a concrete slab, and roughly when it was built. A small kit shed and a 1970s shed with electrical service are very different jobs.

  2. Check permits with the right office

    Unincorporated areas (Big Bear City, Sugarloaf, Fawnskin, Erwin Lake, Baldwin Lake) go through San Bernardino County Land Use Services Building & Safety. Addresses in the City of Big Bear Lake go through the City. If the shed was built with a permit, or is bigger than the county's 120 sq ft no-permit limit, expect to need a demolition permit, and ask before you start.

  3. Disconnect utilities first

    If there's any wiring, a qualified electrician should disconnect it at the source and make it safe. Shut off and cap any water line. Never tear into a wall that might have live wiring.

  4. Empty it, and separate the hazardous items

    Sheds are where paint, stains, fuel, pesticides, pool chemicals and propane cylinders end up. All of those go to the San Bernardino County Fire HHW program, not into the debris pile. Tools, bikes and usable items get sold, donated or moved.

  5. Check for asbestos on older sheds

    San Bernardino County disposal sites require asbestos certification for waste from demolished structures built before 1984, and always for burnt structures. Older roofing, siding and floor tiles are the usual suspects. If it's pre-1984, plan for testing before tear-down.

  6. Take it down top to bottom

    Work from the roof down: roofing, then roof sheathing and rafters, then wall panels, then the frame and floor. Never stand on a weak or snow-loaded roof. Wear gloves, eye protection and boots, and pull or bend nails as you go. Metal kit sheds come apart in reverse of assembly; keep the panels flat because the edges are sharp.

  7. Sort and cut debris to size

    Keep separate piles for clean lumber, treated lumber, metal, roofing and trash. County transfer stations don't accept items over 4 feet long, so cut long boards and panels down. Treated wood is only accepted at the county's Mid-Valley and Victorville sites, not Big Bear.

  8. Haul, then finish the site

    Tarp and tie down every load, because the county doubles the fee for uncovered loads. Pick up nails with a magnet, fill post holes, and decide whether the concrete pad stays. Bare ground next to a cabin is a good chance to improve defensible space.

Disposal rules that catch people out

  • Over 4 feet long: not accepted at county transfer stations (landfills take up to 10 feet). Cut everything down before you load.
  • Treated wood: pressure-treated lumber (common in shed floors, deck framing and posts) is accepted only at the county's Mid-Valley and Victorville sites. California also lists treated wood as hazardous for household disposal, so it can't go in the trash.
  • Asbestos: demolition waste from structures built before 1984 needs asbestos certification at county sites. Friable asbestos goes to specific out-of-county landfills only. We don't handle asbestos.
  • Paint, stains and chemicals: HHW only. Dried-out empty cans are a different case; ask the HHW program.
  • Uncovered loads: double fee at county sites. Tarp and strap.

Deck sections, fencing and hot tub surrounds

The same rules apply to rotted deck boards, small deck sections, short fence runs and the skirting around a hot tub: cut to under 4 feet, separate treated lumber, and check permits for anything structural. If the shed or deck project includes an old spa, read how to get rid of an old hot tub in Big Bear, which covers disconnection and cut-down.

After the shed is gone: fire-safe ground

A shed close to a cabin is part of the defensible-space picture. Once it's gone, rake out the needles and debris that collected under and around it, and don't restack firewood or junk in the same spot against the cabin. Our fire abatement letter guide and weed clearing service cover the standards.

What shed removal costs: the factors

We don't publish prices or invent averages. These are the inputs that set the cost:

  • Size and construction: a resin or metal kit shed comes apart quickly; a framed wood shed with a floor, shingles and a slab takes longer and weighs more.
  • Contents: a full shed is a cleanout and a tear-down in one job.
  • Material mix: treated wood has to go to a different county site; pre-1984 materials may need asbestos testing first.
  • Permits and utilities: permit fees, if any, are set by the City or county, and electrical disconnection is a separate trade.
  • Access: slope, snow, distance to the street and gate width.
  • Disposal fees: county transfer-station fees are by weight (see the county fee table).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to remove a shed in Big Bear City?

Big Bear City is unincorporated, so San Bernardino County Land Use Services applies. The county requires a demolition permit to remove all or part of a permitted building, and lists demolition among the work that needs a permit. Sheds up to 120 sq ft with no utilities don't need a building permit to build. Ask county Building & Safety about your specific shed before you start.

Can I take shed wood to the Big Bear Transfer Station?

Untreated wood cut to under 4 feet, yes, with weight-based fees. Pressure-treated wood is accepted only at the county's Mid-Valley and Victorville sites, and demolition waste from pre-1984 structures needs asbestos certification.

How do I know if my shed has asbestos?

You can't tell by looking. San Bernardino County requires asbestos certification for demolition waste from structures built before 1984, so older sheds with original roofing, siding or floor tiles should be tested before tear-down.

Will you tear down and haul my shed?

For small, non-structural sheds that qualify as minor work (under $1,000 total, no building permit needed), yes, including emptying the contents and hauling everything away. Bigger, structural or permitted structures need a licensed demolition contractor.

What about the concrete slab?

A slab can usually stay, and it makes a handy base for a new shed or a firewood rack kept away from the cabin. Breaking out concrete is a separate, heavier job; ask about it when you get your quote.
