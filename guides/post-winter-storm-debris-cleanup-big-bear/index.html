Post-Winter and Storm Debris Cleanup in Big Bear

Cleaning up after a Big Bear storm or winter: safety, green waste limits, curbside and drop-off rules, the chipping program, fire prep and cost factors.

What do I do with fallen branches in Big Bear?

Stay clear of anything touching a power line and call the utility first. For curbside green waste with Big Bear Disposal (City of Big Bear Lake), cut limbs to under 4 feet long and under 3 inches thick; stumps, dirt, rocks and sod aren't accepted. Big Bear City CSD customers can self-haul green waste to the Big Bear Transfer Station with their Disposal Use Permit at no extra cost, and green carts are only emptied April 1 to October 31. Green waste can't go in the trash cart. Larger storm loads need a roll-off bin or a haul-away service.

Big Bear winters leave the same mess every year: snapped pine limbs, piles of needles and cones, fences pushed over by plow berms, crushed patio furniture and carport frames, and sheds that finally gave up under the snow. Wind events in fall and spring add more. Here is how to clean it up safely, legally and in a way that also gets you ready for fire season.

Step-by-step: storm and winter cleanup

  1. Make it safe before you touch anything

    Stay at least a tree-length away from any limb on or near a power line, and report it to the electric utility (or 911 if it's sparking or blocking a road). Don't walk under hanging or cracked limbs. Standing hazard trees and anything that needs climbing or a chainsaw at height is a job for a tree service.

  2. Photograph the damage

    Take wide and close-up photos of fallen limbs, crushed fencing, carports, decks and roofs before cleanup, with the date. Insurers and property managers ask for them, and they help any contractor quote accurately.

  3. Clear access paths first

    Open the driveway, the path to the door and the area around utility meters and propane tanks. Leave the street clear for plows and emergency vehicles, and don't pile debris in the roadway.

  4. Sort the debris as you go

    Keep four piles: green waste (needles, cones, branches), wood debris from structures (fence boards, deck pieces), bulky items (crushed patio furniture, a collapsed carport) and trash. Each pile has its own rules.

  5. Cut green waste to curbside size

    For Big Bear Disposal curbside service, limbs must be under 4 feet long and under 3 inches in diameter. Bundle or bag what doesn't fit in the green cart according to your provider's rules, and leave stumps, dirt, rocks and sod out.

  6. Use the right drop-off or program

    Big Bear City CSD customers: self-haul green waste to the Big Bear Transfer Station with your Disposal Use Permit at no extra cost, or watch for CSD green-waste drop-off days. City of Big Bear Lake residents with proof of eligibility can use the Clean Bear Site's green-waste bin (no contractor green waste). For big loads, ask Big Bear Disposal about a roll-off bin.

  7. Turn the cleanup into fire prep

    Spring storm debris dries into fire fuel by summer. Once the big pieces are gone, rake needles off roofs, gutters and decks, move firewood away from the cabin, and clear to defensible-space standards before Big Bear Fire's inspection season.

Free chipping: the curbside chipping program

The Mountain Rim Fire Safe Council runs a free curbside chipping program for the Big Bear area, promoted by the Big Bear Fire Department. Registration for 2026 has closed, and the next program begins in June 2027. Check the program page in spring for sign-up dates and stacking rules. Until then, use your green cart, the CSD self-haul option or a haul-away.

Snow-damaged structures and junk

  • Collapsed carports, gazebos and sheds: treat these as debris piles. Cut pieces to under 4 feet for the transfer station and keep treated lumber separate (it only goes to the county's Mid-Valley and Victorville sites). For tear-downs of what's still standing, see shed removal in Big Bear.
  • Broken fencing and deck boards: the same 4-foot and treated-wood rules apply.
  • Patio furniture, grills and hot tub covers: bulky items for your provider's bulky pickup, the transfer station or a haul. Empty and remove propane cylinders first; cylinders go to HHW. A snow-cracked spa? See hot tub removal.
  • Ash from the woodstove: soak it for at least 48 hours before disposal. The Clean Bear Site has an ash bin for eligible city residents.

Spring cleanup is fire season prep

Everything that comes down over winter becomes dry fuel by June. Big Bear Fire inspects properties for defensible space, and a letter can come with a deadline. Clearing storm debris, needles and brush together saves a second trip. Read what to do if you get a fire abatement letter, or see our weed abatement and defensible space service.

Costs: what storm cleanup really costs

We won't quote a fake average. The inputs are:

  • Free capacity you already pay for: your green cart and extra bags (Big Bear Disposal), CSD self-haul with your Disposal Use Permit, and the chipping program when it's running.
  • Roll-off bins: Big Bear Disposal offers roll-offs for storm cleanup and a 4-yard Rent-a-Bin; ask them for current rates.
  • Transfer-station fees for non-green debris: weight-based county fees apply (see the county fee table).
  • Volume, cutting and access: how much is down, how much cutting it needs to meet size limits, and how far it is from the street (slopes, snow, gates).
  • What a tree service has to do first: hazard trees and hanging limbs are a separate trade and a separate price.

With us, the price is confirmed before work starts, from photos or a free on-site look.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put branches in my trash cart in Big Bear?

No. Green waste is banned from the black trash cart in Big Bear City CSD areas (since January 1, 2023, under SB 1383) and goes in green carts or approved drop-offs elsewhere. Use your green cart, the allowed extra bags, a drop-off option or a haul-away.

How long can branches be for Big Bear Disposal green waste pickup?

Under 4 feet long and under 3 inches in diameter. Stumps, dirt, rocks and sod aren't accepted.

When are green carts collected in Big Bear City?

The Big Bear City CSD empties green carts from April 1 to October 31 only. Outside that window, CSD customers can self-haul green waste to the Big Bear Transfer Station with their Disposal Use Permit at no extra cost.

Is there free chipping in Big Bear?

The Mountain Rim Fire Safe Council runs a free curbside chipping program. 2026 registration has closed, and the next program begins in June 2027.

Do you remove fallen trees?

We haul downed branches, limbs and storm debris once they're on the ground and safe to handle. We don't fell, climb or remove standing trees; for hazard trees, hanging limbs or anything near power lines, call a tree service or the utility.

Can you clean up storm debris at a cabin I don't live in?

Yes. Many of our customers are off-mountain owners. We work from a lockbox or gate code and send before-and-after photos. Text photos of the damage to (425) 233-2945 for a quote.
