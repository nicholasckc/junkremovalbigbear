Hot Tub & Spa Removal in Big Bear

Freeze damage, a failed heater or just time for a new one — we take old spas off decks and patios, including tight and tiered mountain decks.

Text photos for a free quoteWhatsApp us

How removal works

  1. Disconnect (you arrange this). A licensed electrician or spa technician shuts off power and caps the plumbing. This must happen before we can start.
  2. Drain. The tub should be drained ahead of time where possible.
  3. Cut down and carry out. Most mountain spas can't leave the way they came in, so the shell is cut into sections and carried out.
  4. Haul away and sweep. Shell, cabinet, cover and equipment go on the truck and the spot is swept clean.

More detail in our guide: what it takes to get a dead spa off a mountain deck.

What affects the price

  • Size — a two-person tub versus a large family spa
  • Access — a ground-level patio versus an upper deck above a slope
  • Construction — cabinet type and how it was installed

Because access varies so much between lots, we quote hot tubs on-site or from several photos showing the path from the tub to the street.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to disconnect the hot tub before removal?

Yes. A licensed electrician or spa technician needs to disconnect power and cap the plumbing first. After that, we handle the cut-down, carry-out and haul-away.

Can you remove a hot tub from an upper or tiered deck?

Usually, yes. The tub is cut into sections and carried down stairs and paths. We confirm when we see the access.

Can you remove a hot tub in winter?

Often, as long as the path from the tub to the truck is clear enough to work safely. Tell us about snow and ice when you book.

How much does hot tub removal cost?

It depends on size, access and construction, so we quote it for free on-site or from photos.
